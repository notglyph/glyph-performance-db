## Three recommended original creative experiments (principles + evidence + tests, never scripts)

1. **Seat-count-closed personal-cost dilemma (Lifeboat, tightened).**
   Principle: same personal stakes + visible vehicles/cost, but the seat/space
   limit is closed in-frame ("one seat each — no laps, no sharing, no second
   trip") so the lap-sitting move can't dissolve the binary. Evidence: Lifeboat
   401K views, 78.9% stayed, lap-sitting dominated the 62 sampled comments.
   Test: stayed% vs Lifeboat's 78.9%; comment coding for lap/split loophole
   share vs Lifeboat's dominant share — a lower share with views/stayed held
   is a pass.
2. **Visible-threat rescue dilemma (Splash, tightened) — carried, still unrun.**
   Principle: a loved one in visible danger from a threat whose rule is shown,
   not told, in the opening — and the rescue method itself must cost something
   visible, so the rock-distraction move stops dissolving the binary. Evidence:
   Splash 86.1% stayed with a threat-rule premise; its dominant loophole was a
   zero-cost distraction. Test: stayed% vs the 82% bar; comment coding for
   distraction-loophole share vs Splash's ~13% (5/38) — a lower share with
   stayed% held above the bar is a pass.
3. **Hidden-detail dilemma (H3's direct test, still unrun).** Principle: one
   findable detail planted in-frame (a number, a name patch, a background
   object) with the CTA pointing at it, giving comments a second engine
   besides the pick. Evidence: no Glyph upload has run a deliberate hidden
   detail; Lifeboat shows loophole-hunting happens even without a planted
   detail (647 comments at 0.16%), so a planted detail + CTA could push the
   rate. Test: comment rate vs the 0.16% Lifeboat / 0.48% Cliff baselines,
   coded for hunting comments vs pick comments; a rate at or above ~1% with
   hunting behavior is the first H3 pass.
