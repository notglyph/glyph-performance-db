## Three recommended original creative experiments (principles + evidence + tests, never scripts)

1. **Identity-copy dilemma (today's convergence).** Principle: open on two
   identical figures in frame zero with one visible tell, and force a choice
   where picking wrong costs the chooser — the dilemma is proof of identity.
   Evidence: fog-doubles story (43K likes, Oct 25), Hiachi as today's Hollow
   anchor, Animal Hospital doppelgangers tomorrow — three identity-copy items
   in one week. Test: stayed% vs the 72.2% D.B. Cooper bar; comments must argue
   the tell, not the premise; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.23%.
2. **Two-way-rule dilemma (The Visitor lesson, Glyph-ized).** Principle: post
   the rule in frame zero, then give the protagonist a costly way to turn the
   rule back on its maker — the dilemma is whether using the loophole is worth
   what it costs. Evidence: The Visitor (220K likes / 350 comments, Oct 24),
   debate axis = the rule's fairness. Test: stayed% vs 72.2%; comment rate vs
   0.23%, coded for rule-fairness debate vs premise confusion.
3. **Rule-demonstrated frame-zero test (H12's direct test).** Principle: the
   whole dilemma — choice + cost — readable from the visuals at 0:00, with no
   reliance on narration for the first 3 seconds. Evidence: the last two Glyph
   frame zeros (empty opening 72.2%, prize-only opening 64.9%) both lost at the
   swipe; competitor winners open on information (Mission 003, 21 videos).
   Test: stayed% vs the 82% bar and vs the 64.9–72.2% range of the last two
   openings; a stayed% above 82% with a visible-dilemma frame is the first
   H12 pass, a sub-65% repeat falsifies the frame-first reading for this channel.
