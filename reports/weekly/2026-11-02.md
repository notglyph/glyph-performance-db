## Three recommended original creative experiments (principles + evidence + tests, never scripts)

1. **Visible-threat rescue dilemma (Splash, tightened).** Principle: a loved
   one in visible danger from a threat whose rule is shown, not told, in the
   opening — and the rescue method itself must cost something visible, so the
   rock-distraction move stops dissolving the binary. Evidence: Splash
   86.1% stayed with a threat-rule premise; its dominant loophole was a
   zero-cost distraction. Test: stayed% vs the 82% bar; comment coding for
   distraction-loophole share vs Splash's ~13% (5/38) — a lower share with
   stayed% held above the bar is a pass.
2. **Clean-timer dilemma (Cliff, clarified).** Principle: one timer, one
   holder, one cost — the timer assignment must survive a first viewing with
   no confusion about who holds what. Evidence: Cliff held 80.7% with a
   dominant mom pick, but several comments confused the 1-day / 10-year
   assignment. Test: stayed% vs Cliff's 80.7%; comment coding for timer
   confusion — fewer confused comments at equal-or-better stayed% is a pass.
3. **Hidden-detail dilemma (H3's direct test, still unrun).** Principle: one
   findable detail planted in-frame (a number, a name patch, a background
   object) with the CTA pointing at it, giving comments a second engine
   besides the pick. Evidence: no Glyph upload has run a deliberate hidden
   detail; the no-detail baseline is 0.22–0.48% comment rates across five
   mined uploads. Test: comment rate vs the 0.48% Cliff / 0.31% Splash
   baselines, coded for hunting comments vs pick comments; a rate at or above
   ~1% with hunting behavior is the first H3 pass.
