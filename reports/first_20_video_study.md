## Phase 4 — Winners vs losers

**Observations (what the sample shows):**
1. **8 of 10 Group A hooks pose an unresolved question or a violated expectation** (impostor, puzzle, pun, missing sock, missing sister, tamed hacker, stalker-or-not, which-is-better). **7 of 8 Group B hooks state a premise without posing a question.** This is the sample's sharpest split.
2. Group A's strongest comment performance (A2: 5,644 comments) comes from a video whose *format is* participation. A10's forced-choice comparison is the YouTube analogue.
3. Within craftee — the only creator with both groups well represented — the breakouts (A5, A6) carry an **emotional arc or an inverted goal**, while the typicals (B1–B4, B8) are **premise statements and rule explorations**.
4. Stakes timing: Group A premises imply a stake in the hook itself (a missing person, an impostor, a hacker). Group B hooks mostly imply a *situation*; the stake, if any, arrives later (Inferred from logged hooks).
5. Twist placement: A5/A8/A9 resolve through **reversal** (enemy→ally, sinister→innocent, separation→reunion). No reversal is logged for any Group B record.

**Hypotheses (not conclusions):**
- H-Q: *A hook that poses a question outperforms the same premise stated flat* — supported by the A/B split; **counterexample:** B1's device ("You Teleport Every Hour") appears elsewhere in the wider log as a winner shape for the same creator, and premise-statement world rules are H5's whole evidence base. The question form may matter most when the premise is *not* already novel to that audience — or craftee's Oct 17–19 run was fatigue, not form.
- H-R: *A mid/late reversal distinguishes breakouts from premise-exploration* — supported within craftee; **counterexample:** A2 (math puzzle) has no story or reversal at all and is the sample's biggest multiple — reversal is one road, participation is another.
- H-P: *Participation formats produce the extreme comment outliers* — A2 + A10 + the wider hidden-detail log (12 instances). No counterexample in the sample; Glyph's own forced-choice dilemmas are the untested local case (their comment rates are currently *low* — 0.21%, which cuts against easy transfer and is itself a finding).

**Recommendations:** test H-Q and H-R as Glyph experiments (Phase 5); treat H-P as "diagnose before investing" — Glyph already runs forced choice, so the gap is in execution details (loophole closure, CTA placement), not format adoption.

**Answers to the mission's ten questions, briefly:** (1) Visually understandable openings — *cannot be answered; frame data Unavailable.* (2) Stakes earlier — yes, in hook text (observation 4). (3) Twists — breakouts log reversals; typicals don't (observation 5). (4) Cause-and-effect escalation — inverted-goal and reversal formats generate it structurally; premise-exploration doesn't require it. (5) Understandable without audio — *cannot be answered without frame/audio inspection.* (6) Participation — yes, the two most extreme engagement results are participation formats. (7) In both groups: world rules, second-person framing, Roblox settings — presence in both means they are **not** differentiators by themselves. (8) Insufficient evidence: any frame-level technique; "first 3 seconds" claims of any kind. (9) Creator styles: craftee = volume + rule-worlds, wins when a rule gets an arc; nepshublox = title-craft (puns, questions) on gameplay-adjacent skits; fearless.gman = ambiguity/violation storytime; roblxify = participation formats. (10) Transfers to Glyph: question-form hooks, reversal-bearing dilemmas, participation mechanics — Phase 5.

*No causal or statistically significant claims are made; n=18, observational, one platform dominant.*

## Phase 5 — Three Glyph experiments

Glyph context applied: 30–45s targets; the Theft Parachute → D.B. Cooper split (stayed ~58%→71.3% vs avg viewed ~101–104%) says our losses happen at the swipe while the body holds — so Experiment 1 attacks frame one, Experiment 2 attacks the body's ceiling, Experiment 3 attacks the 0.21% comment-rate floor.

### Experiment 1 — Visual hook: "The Whole Dilemma In Frame One"
- **Principle:** viewers swipe before the dilemma registers; a frame that already contains both options, the danger, and the constraint removes the comprehension delay. (H2/H10.)
- **Supporting evidence:** Glyph's own split signature across two videos; Group A's question/violation hooks (8/10) all front-load the unresolved element.
- **Contradictory evidence:** no verified frame data exists for any sample video — the frame claim rests on Glyph's metrics + logged hook text, not on inspected competitor frames. D.B. Cooper's re-cut may already have done this and still settled at 71.3% — which would weaken the hypothesis (and is exactly what this test discriminates).
- **Concept (original):** "The Lifeboat Dilemma" — frame one: a sinking cartoon ship's lifeboat, two characters already seated, a third in the water reaching up, and the boat visibly cracking under the current weight. No title card, no setup shot.
- **First-frame directions:** three characters visible; the constraint readable without audio (crack + waterline); the reaching hand creates the question; bright hull against dark water for contrast.
- **Structure (BUT/THEREFORE):** you pull him in → BUT the boat dips lower → THEREFORE someone must lighten it → BUT the lightest is holding the only flare → climax choice, CTA at the decision point.
- **Metric:** stayed% and the stayed/avg-viewed split vs D.B. Cooper (71.3% / 101.4%) and Zero Gravity (81.6%).
- **Weakening result:** stayed% ≤ ~74% with avg viewed still >100% → frame-one comprehension was not the binding constraint; the premise-ceiling explanation gains.

### Experiment 2 — Storytelling: "Every Accident Causes The Next"
- **Principle:** BUT/THEREFORE escalation chains manufacture a new question every few seconds; the sample's within-creator split (arc/inversion breakouts vs premise-exploration typicals) points the same way.
- **Supporting evidence:** A5/A6 vs B1–B4/B8 (same creator); the wider escalation-chain log (H4, 8+ instances, 3 creators).
- **Contradictory evidence:** A2's puzzle — no story at all — beat every story in the sample. Escalation is a road, not the road. Also craftee fatigue confound noted above.
- **Concept (original):** "The New Kid Accidentally Became Mayor" — each attempt to undo the accident legally promotes him further (already briefed to ChatGPT Oct 19 as the president storyline; this experiment formalizes its test).
- **First-frame directions:** a kid at a comically oversized mayor's desk, sash already on, holding a stamped form he clearly doesn't understand — status violation readable in one frame.
- **Structure:** crowned by accident → BUT he tries to resign → THEREFORE the resignation form makes him governor → BUT he hides → THEREFORE hiding during a crisis makes him a hero → climax: one forced choice with the CTA.
- **Metric:** stayed% + avg % viewed together (the chain should lift the body, not just the open), vs the channel's recent median.
- **Weakening result:** avg viewed rises but stayed% stays ≤75% → chains help holders, not stoppers; pair with Experiment 1 or drop.

### Experiment 3 — Engagement: "One Findable Lie"
- **Principle:** comments are a distribution engine when viewers are given something *specific* to catch; Glyph's dilemmas currently produce forced-choice comments at only ~0.21% because the choice alone gives viewers little to discover.
- **Supporting evidence:** roblxify hidden-detail pattern (12 instances in the wider log; murder-alibi variant: 566 comments); A2's participation outlier (5,644 comments).
- **Contradictory evidence:** H-P's transfer problem — Glyph already runs forced choice and comments are low; the hidden detail may not rescue a dilemma whose loophole is open. Oxygen Mask's top comments invented a cheat instead of debating — details must be *findable*, not *inventable*.
- **Concept (original):** "The Two Keys Dilemma" — two identical rescue keys, one is visibly (on close look) stamped with the wrong room number; the spoken CTA asks which key — and whether anyone noticed the stamp.
- **First-frame directions:** both keys large in frame, stamp visible but not highlighted; the dilemma (two doors, one flooding corridor) readable behind them.
- **Structure:** rule stated → BUT only one key is real → THEREFORE checking costs time → BUT the water is rising → climax choice; CTA names the choice, not the detail (let viewers discover it).
- **Metric:** comment rate vs the 0.21–0.22% no-detail baseline; share of comments mentioning the stamp.
- **Weakening result:** comment rate <0.4% or stamp mentions <10% of comments → hidden details don't transfer to dilemma format; retire the mechanic for Glyph.
