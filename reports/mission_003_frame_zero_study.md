## What the evidence supports (hypotheses, not laws)

1. **Frame zero must carry information.** Every verified winner opening shows the premise, a contradiction, a threat, or a choice — the only content-free openings in 21 videos belong to losers (Xenost B2/B3), and Glyph's own underperformer (D.B. Cooper) is the soft version: characters present, dilemma absent. (H12, strengthened.)
2. **The extreme top fuses choice + stakes + participation in one frame.** The sample's #1 video (19M, 9 seconds) puts the CTA words in the opening frame as the choice labels, over a character hanging above a fire. Nothing else in the sample combines all three at 0:00.
3. **Escalation and twists do not separate winners from losers** — losers escalate and twist at the same rate. They are retention table stakes; the separation happens at the open and, at the top end, in participation.
4. **Counterexamples that bind us:** identical opening formula, 10x outcome swing (MiAnimation1 "Which X" set); a visual gag + escalation chain at 0.08x (IQ pyramids); a shock opening + explicit CTA at 0.09x (First PERIOD); a perfect contradiction opening at 0.58x (Xenost grandpa). No single frame technique is sufficient — subject/pairing choice is a first-order variable we keep under-weighting.

## §9 — Three first-frame experiments (original concepts, production-ready specs)

### Experiment A — Instant comprehension: "The Elevator Dilemma"
- **Premise:** A stuck elevator, a snapping cable, an overweight load — and one beloved heavy thing that has to go.
- **Frame-zero directions (9:16, for Naieem):** Interior corner angle. Ceiling hatch open: cable strands visibly parted, one strand mid-snap. Red lit sign: "OVERLOAD". Two characters: one clutching a big gold crate to his chest, the other pointing at the crate, then the doors. Floor counter showing a high floor. Everything readable muted: danger (cable), constraint (OVERLOAD), choice (crate vs. ride down).
- **First 3 seconds:** 0–1s a strand pops with a spark; 1–2s the elevator lurches and both characters look up; 2–3s the narrator's question lands (suggested line for ChatGPT to finalize: a "would you drop it?" question form) as the crate-holder hugs it tighter.
- **Rationale:** F1 + F3 — premise, danger, and constraint in one frame; mirrors Xenost A1's prop-carried premise (live mic + F paper) in Glyph's dilemma grammar.
- **Contradictory evidence:** readable openings also lost (MiAnimation1 B group) — comprehension may be necessary, not sufficient; the subject (what the heavy thing IS) is the co-variable.
- **Production difficulty:** LOW–MEDIUM (one set, two characters, one hero prop).
- **Hypothesis:** a fully readable frame zero lifts stayed% toward the 82% bar. **Metric:** stayed% + the stayed/avg-viewed split vs D.B. Cooper (71.3/101.4). **Weakening result:** stayed ≤74% with avg viewed >100%.

### Experiment B — Visual contradiction: "The Mayor" (escalation chain)
- **Premise:** A kid accidentally becomes mayor and every attempt to resign promotes him (the Oct 19 escalation brief, formalized as the contradiction test).
- **Frame-zero directions:** A small kid in an oversized mayor's sash, swallowed by a giant leather desk chair, holding a stamped form he clearly can't read; two adult officials lean in waiting for HIS decision. One frame, one impossibility.
- **First 3 seconds:** 0–1s the gavel is placed in his hand by an adult; 1–2s he stamps the wrong form; 2–3s the room erupts — the accident chain starts.
- **Rationale:** F4 — contradiction openings went 4/4 in Xenost's winners (statue vs kid; hair flying off). The sash/desk staging is Glyph-original, not an imitation of any sampled video.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Xenost's grandpa contradiction opened perfectly and still did 0.58x; contradiction buys the stop, not the stay — the chain has to deliver (F6 says escalation is table stakes, so it must be *causal*, beat-to-beat).
- **Production difficulty:** MEDIUM (office set, 3–4 characters, reusable).
- **Hypothesis:** contradiction frame zero + causal chain lifts both stayed% and avg viewed. **Metric:** stayed% + avg viewed together. **Weakening result:** avg viewed rises but stayed ≤75% (chain holds holders, doesn't stop swipers).

### Experiment C — Question plus consequence: "The Two Levers Dilemma"
- **Premise:** One lever saves your friend, the other saves you — and one of the dangers isn't real.
- **Frame-zero directions:** POV-ish three-quarter shot: the protagonist's hand hovering between a red and a green lever. Behind glass on the left, his friend stands on a visibly cracking platform over a drop; behind glass on the right, the protagonist's own avatar on an identical platform. Both consequences are IN the frame. Findable detail: the right platform's "cracks" are painted on (brush strokes visible on close look).
- **First 3 seconds:** 0–1s a crack spreads on the left platform; 1–2s the friend pounds the glass; 2–3s narration poses the choice (question form) — without mentioning the paint.
- **Rationale:** F5 + F8 + the sample's #1 video (choice labels + visible stakes at 0:00) fused with Mission 002's hidden-detail comment engine; the CTA sits at the climax asking which lever — viewers who spotted the paint get to say so.
- **Contradictory evidence:** participation formats on MiAnimation1 also produced a 0.52x (Shakira) — the pairing/subject still matters; and Glyph's forced choice currently yields only 0.21% comments, so transfer is genuinely uncertain.
- **Production difficulty:** MEDIUM (split set, glass effect, one detail prop).
- **Hypothesis:** visible dual consequences + a findable fake lift comment rate off the floor while holding stayed%. **Metrics:** comment rate vs the 0.21% baseline; share of comments spotting the painted crack; stayed%. **Weakening result:** comment rate <0.4% or paint mentions <10% of comments.
