## Strongest hooks (current register)

- **Judged-sort world rule (NEW, strongest new signal):** at a stated age or moment, everyone is sorted by their deeds into visible kinds — the sort itself is the cost, and the judgment must be shown in frame zero (a deed, a glow, a mark), not explained later. Both sides of the sort must cost something or it reads as lore.
- **Countable-life world rule (NEW):** a number above every head that only goes down — and a market that lets the rich buy what the poor must sell. The counter must be visible in frame zero; the dilemma is what you trade to keep it up, not that it exists.
- **Single-rule world with a visible cost (carried, strengthened to 3 instances):** state one rule in frame zero AND show what breaking — or obeying — it costs in the same frame. A rule alone is a premise; a rule plus a cost is a dilemma.

## Three recommended experiments (principles, never scripts)

1. **Judged-sort world rule (NEW — strongest new lead).** Principle: open on the sorting moment itself — a deed shown in frame zero, a visible mark or glow that sorts the character — then force a choice where the sort is the trap (the kind you earned helps you but costs someone else, or the kind you want requires a deed you won't do). The dilemma is which sort to live with, not what the kinds are. Evidence: ohcookii species-at-18 story at 86K likes / 100 comments on 3.7K followers (Oct 30); cipher breaths-counter world at 27K likes (Oct 30); noirfilms sleep rule at 122.6K likes (Oct 28) — three independent single-rule instances at scale in four days. Uncertainty: all caption-level, videos not watched, likes only with no retention data; whether the judgment is shown in frame zero or narrated later is Unverified; single-rule comprehension (H5) is moderate, performance guarantee low. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing which sort to take, not asking what the kinds are.
2. **Countable-life world with a market (NEW).** Principle: open on a number above every head that only ticks down — and a market where the rich buy what the poor must sell — then force a choice where keeping your number up costs someone else's (buy, sell, or steal breaths/time/lives). The dilemma is whose number you trade, not that numbers exist. Evidence: cipher_shorts breaths-counter world (27K likes / 23 comments, 36K followers, Oct 30) — visible counter, buy/sell asymmetry, stolen-breath facility, return-all button. Uncertainty: caption-level only, video not watched; likes only, no retention; the return-all button risks a no-cost resolution if copied (a fix must cost something). Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing buy vs sell vs steal, not the premise.
3. **Balanced three-option choice — every option carries a shown cost (NEW, counter-experiment).** Principle: open on three options in frame zero, EACH with its catch shown in the same frame — including the attractive one — then force a choice where the catch is the decision (which catch to live with), not the prize. The dilemma is which cost to take, not which prize to want. Evidence: the unbalanced counter-instances — team_deam's no-downside third monster (1.6K likes / 150 comments, Oct 31) and Pulse's strictly-better treehouse (710/63, Oct 31) — both drew comments onto an obvious pick rather than a trade-off; Glyph's Lucky Coin lesson supplies the frame-zero-catch requirement. Uncertainty: comment counts are small-account signals, not retention data; whether balanced options actually convert comments into debate (vs agreement) is untested on Glyph. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing which catch to take — if comments converge on one option, the balance failed.
