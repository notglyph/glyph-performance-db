## Three recommended experiments (principles, never scripts)

1. **Scheduled-payback rule (NEW — freshest lead).** Principle: open on a visible calendar or clock that shows exactly when an act returns to its actor — then force a choice where the act you need most is the one whose payback you can least afford (help someone now and be hurt tomorrow, hurt someone now and be helped tomorrow). The dilemma is which dated payback to accept, not whether payback exists. Evidence: karma-calendar rule (Nov 3, 120K likes / 470 comments — whatever you do returns in 24 hours, tracked on a visible calendar). Uncertainty: single instance, summary-level, video not watched, likes only with no retention data; whether the calendar is visible in frame zero or narrated later is Unverified; a payback that lands off-screen risks a no-cost resolution. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing which payback to accept, not asking how the calendar works.
2. **Fear-sustains-the-system world rule (NEW).** Principle: open on a rule everyone obeys and a visible structure that only makes sense if the rule holds (a ladder, a wall, a line) — then let one defector break the rule on camera and show what the rule was hiding (the system runs on shared fear, the keeper, the watching audience). The dilemma is whether to expose the mechanism or keep climbing once you see it. Evidence: ladder world (Nov 2, 140K likes / 270 comments — three whispered rules, a defector crosses to the empty side, the climb runs on shared fear); walled town (Nov 1, 150K likes / 580 comments — a climber reaches the top and finds a watching city, closing on warn-vs-escape). Uncertainty: both summary-level, videos not watched, likes only, no retention; whether the defection lands early or late is Unverified; a reveal with no choice attached reads as lore, not a dilemma. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing expose vs obey, not asking how life works on the structure.
3. **Hidden-audience choice (NEW).** Principle: open inside a closed world with one rule that keeps it closed — then reveal the world is watched (a ceiling sky, a lamp sun, a live audience) and force the choice the reveal creates: warn the people inside and lose your own way out, or escape alone and leave them watched. The dilemma is warn vs escape, not the reveal itself. Evidence: walled town (Nov 1, 150K likes / 580 comments — sky-as-ceiling, sun-as-lamp, city chanting the climber's name, closing question "would you climb back down and warn them?"). Uncertainty: single instance for this exact shape, summary-level, video not watched, likes only, no retention; the warn-vs-escape choice must be shown in-frame, not just asked in narration, or it reads as a sequel hook. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing warn vs escape, not requesting Part 2.
