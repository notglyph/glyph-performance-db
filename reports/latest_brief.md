## Three recommended experiments (principles, never scripts)

1. **Scheduled-payback rule (STRENGTHENED — fastest-moving lead, now a spreading template).** Principle: open on a visible calendar or clock that shows exactly when an act returns to its actor — then force a choice where the act you need most is the one whose payback you can least afford (help someone now and be hurt tomorrow, hurt someone now and be helped tomorrow). The dilemma is which dated payback to accept, not whether payback exists. Evidence: karma-calendar rule (Nov 3, grew 120K likes / 470 comments → 310K likes / 1.1K comments in ~24h on a 620-follower account; now retold by 7+ accounts Nov 3–4; a second scheduled-payback premise, mean-words echoing back 12 hours later, surfaced Nov 3). Uncertainty: instances are summary-level, videos not watched, likes only with no retention data; whether the calendar is visible in frame zero or narrated later is Unverified; the copy-wave means a Glyph entry now competes with copies, so differentiation is untested. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing which payback to accept, not asking how the calendar works.
2. **Defector weaponizes the rule (NEW).** Principle: open on a rule everyone obeys and a powerful figure demanding the hero use it for them — then let the hero turn the rule's own logic against its enforcers (ban the word that disarms them, ask the question the rule forbids). The dilemma is which use of the rule to choose when obeying hands the powerful a weapon and defying costs the hero everything. Evidence: ban-a-word (Nov 3, @dogeoki, 22K likes / 40 comments on 1.4K followers — hero bans "obey" instead of "retreat"); smart-banned school (Nov 4, @ralphanimations, 74K likes / 170 comments — hero defeats robot copies by asking the banned question). Uncertainty: two instances, both summary-level, videos not watched, likes only, no retention; whether the weaponizing turn lands early enough to hold the swipe is Unverified; a defection with no cost attached reads as a power fantasy, not a dilemma. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing obey vs defy, not asking how the rule works.
3. **Cost-first opening in a flooded lane (carried).** Principle: in a format the audience now calls flooded, skip the rule explanation — open mid-cost (a payback landing, a touch burning, a number dropping) so the viewer feels the price before hearing the rule, then reveal the rule as the explanation of the cost. The dilemma must still be a real choice with a shown cost on every option, not just a striking image. Evidence: Nov 4 flood-fatigue compilation (audience complaint that "In this world…" clips have flooded FYPs, cycling many creators' rules); Nov 3 live-action "In this town" remake (300K likes / 990 comments — the format is now recognizable enough to parody); karma rule's acceleration shows cost-driven premises still move while plain rule explainers pile up. Uncertainty: two fatigue instances, both summary-level, engagement Unverified on the compilation; whether cost-first openings actually retain better than rule-first ones is untested on Glyph side; a cost without a legible rule risks confusion rather than curiosity. Measurable test: stayed% vs the 82% breakout bar; comment rate vs Lucky Coin's 0.24%, with comments arguing the choice, not asking what the rule is.
